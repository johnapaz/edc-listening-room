What’s supported

  • Keyboard. Every link and button can be reached with Tab and used with Enter (or Space for buttons), with a clearly visible focus outline. A Skip to content link is the first stop on every page.
  • Screen readers. Pages use real headings, labeled search and filters, and buttons that announce whether a day is selected. When you search or change the day, the number of results is announced.
  • Clear link names. Links that open a new tab, like tracks on YouTube, announce it.
  • Touch. Buttons and links are at least 44 pixels tall, a comfortable size to tap.
  • Small screens and zoom. Layouts reflow down to the narrow outer screen of a folding phone (about 280 pixels wide) and at 200% zoom, without sideways scrolling.
  • Reduced motion. If your device is set to reduce motion, animations are turned off.
  • Contrast. Text is light on a dark background and is designed for comfortable contrast.

Tips

  • Press Tab once after a page loads to reveal the {{ui Skip to content}} link.
  • On the lineup, the day buttons work as toggle buttons; their selected state is announced.
  • Your browser’s text zoom and reader settings work as usual.

Known gaps

  • Track artwork is decorative; the track title and artist name always carry the meaning. Thumbnails come from YouTube and have no detailed descriptions.
  • There’s no light theme yet. If the dark design is hard for you to read, your device’s high-contrast or invert settings will still work.
  • Keyboard use, zoom and small-screen layouts have been checked in browsers, but a complete hands-on pass with every major screen reader hasn’t been finished yet.

Something not working?

If anything is hard to use, tell the person who shared Listening Room with you. Accessibility problems are treated as bugs.
