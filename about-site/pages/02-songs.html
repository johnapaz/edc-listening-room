The rule: one exact recording, checked by a person

Before a song appears, someone confirms:

  • It’s the right artist. Many artists share similar names, so identity is checked first.
  • It’s the right track. The YouTube video matches the released song.
  • It’s the right version, or the app says when it might not be (see below).
  • It plays on YouTube through a direct video link, not a search.

Only songs that pass are published. Automatic matches and search results are never shown as songs.

About the artwork

Pictures next to songs are the YouTube thumbnails for those exact videos, loaded directly from YouTube. They aren’t artist portraits, and Listening Room never uses another artist’s image or makes one up. When there’s no reviewed video, or a thumbnail can’t load, you’ll see the artist’s initials instead.

Why not more songs?

Checking recordings by hand is slower than grabbing the top search result, but it means every song you tap is the one you were promised. Coverage grows as songs pass review; check back before the festival.
